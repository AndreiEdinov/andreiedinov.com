PFAS in Georgia: where we stand

The European Court of Auditors has published a report on the EU approach to PFAS. I read it as an engineer working in Georgia: what the EU already requires, what Georgian rules cover, and where the gaps are.

November 21, 2026 · PFAS, Drinking water, Regulation

Why I looked at this

On 18 November the European Court of Auditors published a report on the EU approach to PFAS. I read it as an engineer working in Georgia (the country).

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are a group of about 10,000 synthetic chemicals, according to the auditors. The carbon-fluorine bond makes them very persistent. The US EPA says exposure to certain levels of PFAS may lead to lower fertility, developmental effects in children, a weaker vaccine response and a higher risk of some cancers.

Two points from the report and the EU rules

  • Since 12 January 2026, EU drinking water must meet PFAS limits: 100 ng/l (0.1 µg/l) for the sum of 20 listed PFAS or 500 ng/l (0.5 µg/l) for PFAS Total. Member states apply one or both.
  • On 19 November an ECHA committee backed an EU-wide PFAS restriction with derogations for specific uses. The Commission and member states decide next.

For scale: 1 ng/l is 0.001 µg/l, one millionth of a milligram per litre. In the US, the EPA’s 2024 rule set 4 ng/l each for PFOA and PFOS.
