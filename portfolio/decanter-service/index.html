GEA decanter repair: vibration cut from 18–20 to 4–5 mm/s in one working day

Repaired a GEA waterMaster CF 4000 decanter at a food plant wastewater facility: the centrifuge was running with vibration of 18–20 mm/s. We stripped the bowl and scroll, replaced worn parts and reassembled the machine in one working day. After the repair vibration dropped to 4–5 mm/s.

02

Engineering challenge

Centrifuge vibration had reached 18–20 mm/s, the GEA warning and shutdown thresholds. The task was to find and remove the cause and return the decanter to operation.

03

Solution

Repair with full disassembly: bearings, seals, drive shaft and belts replaced from a 27-item parts list, deposits cleaned off the scroll, wear checked, units reassembled to the manufacturer drawing. In the GEA troubleshooting table, scroll deposits and bearing wear are causes of excessive vibration; the repair removed both. The sequence is shown below.

    Bowl and scroll lifted out with the overhead hoist and stripped to the assembly drawing.

  3. 03

    Parts replacement

    27 items

    Bearings, shaft seals, drive shaft, narrow V-belt set, inductive speed sensor.

  4. 04

    Cleaning and assembly

    wear check

    Deposits cleaned off the scroll, parts checked for wear, units reassembled in reverse order. Speed sensor gap and belt tension set with gauges.

  5. 05

    Start-up

    4–5 mm/s

    Test run with the built-in vibration sensors: 4–5 mm/s, in the GEA “good” range (up to 7 mm/s). The centrifuge went back into operation.

Time

The whole job took one working day, 14 hours, with a crew of three.

Service engineer. Led a crew of two mechanics. Bowl and scroll disassembly, replacement of bearings, seals, drive shaft and belts, cleaning deposits off the scroll, wear inspection, reassembly, start-up and vibration check.
