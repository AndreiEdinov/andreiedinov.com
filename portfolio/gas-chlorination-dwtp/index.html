Gas chlorination for the upgrade of two drinking water plants

I am designing a gas chlorination system for the upgrade of plants built in the 1970s–80s. The plants treat 400,000 and 550,000 m³/day.

Industry
Drinking water
Technologies
Gas chlorine, Disinfection
Status
In progress, launch 2027
01

Input data

Plant capacities400,000 and 550,000 m³/day
Reagentliquid chlorine in 1 t containers
My roleProject process engineer
02

Engineering challenge

The plants were built in the 1970s–80s, and the chlorine system has to be replaced. The new system must hold the set dose accurately, leave headroom for capacity growth and stay safe along the whole chlorine path, from storage to injection into the water.

03

Solution

A vacuum gas chlorination system with standby at every stage, from the container store to the ejectors at the injection points. The diagram below shows how it works.

04

Result

The solution step by step: chlorine path from storage to water

  1. 01

    Storage

    1 t containers

    Two container racks on scales with automatic changeover. Stock sized for operation at maximum dose.

  2. 02

    Liquid chlorine feed

    liquid phase

    Liquid chlorine piping in seamless heavy-wall steel (SCH 80 / ASTM A106 Gr. B), motorised valves and pressure monitoring. Overpressure protection on the lines.

  3. 03

    Vaporisation and pressure reduction

    duty + standby

    Electric liquid chlorine vaporisers, gas filter, pressure reducing and relief valves.

  4. 04

    Vacuum dosing

    under vacuum

    Vacuum regulators with standby and shut-off on vacuum loss. Pre-oxidation chlorinators dose in proportion to water flow; disinfection chlorinators are controlled by residual chlorine analysers.

  5. 05

    Ejection and injection

    on motive water

    Ejectors, a flowmeter and check valves on every line. Chlorine solution is injected at the primary (pre-oxidation) and secondary (final disinfection) chlorination points.

Safety

Two-level Cl₂ gas monitoring that instantly triggers automatic emergency shut-off at the containers. Once a leak is contained, emergency ventilation starts and sends the gas in cascade to wet NaOH scrubbers for neutralisation.

Control

Control panels (PLC + HMI) integrated into the plant SCADA. Dosing is automatic: flow-paced at pre-oxidation, controlled by residual chlorine analysers at disinfection.

My role

Project process engineer: process and hydraulic calculations, equipment selection, P&ID development, specifications, design of emergency neutralisation systems (scrubbers), control algorithms for the automation system and technical support of the 3D modelling. Then active involvement in installation supervision and commissioning.
